## 0. Project Overview

### Research objective

This project studies **parameter-based multi-task learning for speech** using fixed wavCSE/WavLM embeddings. The three downstream tasks are:

- **KS — Keyword Spotting:** 12 classes;
- **SI — Speaker Identification:** 1,251 classes;
- **ER — Emotion Recognition:** 4 classes.

The immediate objective is to find a Task Relation Learning method that produces a reproducible improvement over the matched wavCSE baseline without a material regression on KS, SI, or ER. The larger scientific objective is to explain the chain:

```text
task/data properties
    ↓
task relationships
    ↓
transfer and optimization behaviour
    ↓
appropriate parameter-based MTL mechanism
```

The upstream WavLM representation is fixed. Architecture comparisons must therefore isolate the relation mechanism rather than silently changing pooling, layers, data, optimization, or evaluation.

### Starting method and reference

- **Reference/champion:** matched wavCSE baseline.
- **Formal Task Relation Learning baseline:** classical MTRL, which learns a symmetric task covariance matrix Ω from task-head parameter summaries.
- **Established pre-week position:** classical MTRL had been evaluated at 16 and 25 layers, over five seeds and ER LOSO, without a reproducible meaningful advantage over wavCSE.
- **Critical methodological constraints:** one seed screens but does not confirm; pooling must be matched; serious ER claims require speaker-independent LOSO; test results are not used for hyperparameter selection.

### Why this week mattered

Before this week, the project knew that MTRL did not win, but did not know **why**. This week converted several plausible explanations into controlled diagnostic conclusions, negative results, and a concrete benchmark direction.

## 3. Studies Conducted

### DG-0001 — Empirical directed task-transfer matrix

**Question:**  
Does useful KS/SI/ER transfer have a directional or pair-selective structure that a single symmetric dense Ω cannot represent?

**Method:**  
Ran matched single-task, pairwise, and three-task wavCSE arms with `smp(0.5)`, all 25 layers, seed 42, and a fixed final-epoch checkpoint. Candidate ER-directed effects were followed with ten-fold speaker-independent LOSO. Because the pair arms changed update counts and effective ER minibatch size, two step-matched ER-only LOSO controls and five-epoch reverse controls were added.

**Scale:**  
14 completed MLflow runs: 7 Stage-A task-set arms, 3 LOSO arms, and 4 optimization controls. The five LOSO arms executed 50 held-out-speaker folds in total. All scientific runs recorded Study ID, stage, task set, seed, method, pooling, layers, commit SHA, and a DagsHub note.

**Result:**  
The raw LOSO differences were very large: ER←KS `+0.3411` and ER←SI `+0.2794`, each positive in `10/10` folds. These did **not** identify semantic transfer. ER-only update-matched controls reproduced almost all of the apparent gain:

- KS+ER minus step-matched ER: `+0.0053`, 95% CI `[−0.0289, +0.0394]`;
- SI+ER minus step-matched ER: `−0.0589`, 95% CI `[−0.0883, −0.0295]`.

The exposure control explained about 98% of the raw KS-associated gain and more than the full raw SI-associated gain, causing the latter residual to reverse sign.

**Decision:**  
**Strong diagnostic conclusion; primary asymmetry hypothesis weakened.** The raw directed matrix is rejected as a semantic-transfer target. Beneficial asymmetry was not demonstrated.

**Meaning:**  
Equal epochs do not imply equal optimization opportunity when task-set size changes. Future transfer studies must control optimizer steps, effective per-task batch size, loss scaling, epoch budget, and checkpoint policy before attributing an effect to task semantics. Stable or saturated Ω magnitude is also not sufficient evidence of useful transfer.

**Evidence:** [`DG-0001 analysis`](../studies/DG-0001/analysis.md), [`result.json`](../studies/DG-0001/result.json), commits `4c0f08a`, `330d0f7`, `3f4752a`.

### DG-0002 — Exposure-controlled gradient compatibility baseline

**Question:**  
Do KS, SI, and ER exert persistent conflicting, norm-dominant, or phase-dependent gradients on shared parameters, and does classical MTRL's Ω represent or mitigate those interactions?

**Method:**  
Instrumented matched wavCSE and classical MTRL training. At the first, final, and every twentieth optimizer step, the runs measured each task's shared-parameter gradient norm, pairwise cosine, negative-conflict frequency, valid-example count, and training phase. The one-seed screen was followed by matched baseline/MTRL confirmation at seeds `0–4`; seed-level phase summaries, not correlated within-run samples, were the independent observations.

**Scale:**  
12 completed MLflow runs: 2 screening arms plus 10 confirmation runs. Every confirmation run contained 142 diagnostic samples over 2,820 optimizer steps and used the same 1,550,800 shared parameters. All five baseline/MTRL seed pairs passed exposure checks.

**Result:**  
ER dominated the smallest task's norm in every confirmation seed:

|Method|Middle max/min ratio|Late max/min ratio|Seeds ≥ 3 in both phases|
|---|---:|---:|---:|
|wavCSE baseline|`7.243 ± 0.272`|`8.962 ± 0.456`|5/5|
|MTRL|`7.129 ± 0.421`|`9.004 ± 1.059`|5/5|

MTRL-minus-baseline paired intervals included zero in both phases. No pair met the pre-registered persistent-conflict threshold in any seed; late mean cosines remained near zero. Ω magnitude saturated near `1/3` in all five seeds, while seed 4 flipped both ER-edge signs.

**Decision:**  
**CONFIRMED diagnostic** for gradient-norm dominance and MTRL non-mitigation. **REJECTED** for persistent pairwise gradient conflict. No architecture was promoted.

**Meaning:**  
Classical MTRL's head-parameter covariance neither represents nor regulates the dominant shared-gradient scale signal under this protocol. That signal was a concrete optimization observation, not yet a causal explanation of MTRL's outcome null and not an ER performance result.

**Evidence:** [`DG-0002 analysis`](../studies/DG-0002/analysis.md), [`confirmation_result.json`](../studies/DG-0002/confirmation_result.json), MLflow experiment `taskrelation-diagnostics`, confirmation commit `7f6d5248`.

### LT-0001 — Scale- and reliability-aware task relation literature gate

**Question:**  
Is there a published explicit Task Relation Learning method that directly addresses the F9 scale/reliability signal, remains in category, and can be implemented faithfully for disjoint heterogeneous deep classification heads?

**Method:**  
Eight primary sources were evaluated against pre-registered gates: explicit relation object, direct scale/reliability mechanism, formal Task Relation Learning taxonomy, heterogeneous-head applicability, fixed-representation compatibility, and no drift into loss weighting, gradient surgery, low-rank, clustering, or decomposition.

**Scale:**  
8 primary sources; 0 GPU runs; 0 MLflow runs.

**Result:**  
No candidate passed all gates. Explicit covariance/relation methods did not directly regulate heterogeneous shared-gradient scale or required aligned Gaussian outputs. GradNorm and homoscedastic uncertainty weighting directly addressed scale but learned per-task scalar weights rather than relations. The recent informative-relation method crossed the decomposition boundary.

**Decision:**  
**REJECTED.** The hypothesis that an immediately implementable published explicit relation method directly solves F9 was falsified for the reviewed literature.

**Meaning:**  
Relation magnitude, relation confidence/noise, and optimization weight are distinct quantities. A scale-balancing method cannot be relabelled as Task Relation Learning merely because it addresses the observed symptom.

**Evidence:** [`LT-0001 analysis`](../studies/LT-0001/analysis.md), [`result.json`](../studies/LT-0001/result.json), [`literature index`](../literature/INDEX.md).

### DG-0005 — ER data-regime gradient-scale control

**Question:**  
Is ER's large shared-gradient norm task-intrinsic, or is it produced by ER contributing far fewer examples to each mixed batch?

**Method:**  
Compared a matched wavCSE control (A0) with a training-only ER-weighted sampler (A1). A1 raised ER's sampled share from approximately `47/2048` to `435/2048`, while preserving data splits, `num_samples`, 2,820 optimizer steps, 30 epochs, global batch 2,048, diagnostics, and evaluation. A one-seed screen was confirmed at seeds `0–4`.

**Scale:**  
12 accepted completed MLflow runs: 2 screening arms and 10 matched confirmation runs. All five seed pairs passed exposure checks. Two excluded setup attempts were retained in the record; one A1 attempt failed before the first optimizer step because IEMOCAP was subset-wrapped, then the source bug was fixed before accepted runs.

**Result:**  

|Phase|A0 standard mixture|A1 ER-weighted|Paired A1−A0|95% CI|
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
|Middle|`7.243 ± 0.272`|`2.818 ± 0.272`|`−4.425`|`[−4.980, −3.869]`|
|Late|`8.962 ± 0.456`|`2.352 ± 0.270`|`−6.610`|`[−7.353, −5.867]`|

A1's late ratio was below the pre-registered `3.0` threshold in `5/5` seeds. The change was localized to ER: late ER norm `6.972 → 1.608` (about a 77% reduction), while KS moved `+0.066` and SI `−0.117`. Pairwise cosines stayed near zero.

A pre-registered post-hoc bound using existing artifacts found that estimator-size scaling can explain the middle-phase drop but at most `0.758` `[0.715, 0.801]` of the late log-drop. At least about 20% of the late drop therefore requires a smaller mean gradient or noise growing faster than `1/√n`. This bound is exploratory and was not promoted as a new confirmed finding.

**Decision:**  
**CONFIRMED diagnostic.** Gradient scale is a training-mixture property under this representation; the task-intrinsic-scale rationale was withdrawn. No ER performance claim and no architecture promotion.

**Meaning:**  
The strongest F9 interpretation changed. ER is not intrinsically a 7–9× larger-gradient task; its estimated norm depends on how many ER examples construct the batch gradient. Sampling regime must be reported whenever task-gradient scale is discussed. The remaining late-phase component is narrower: measure the pool-mean gradient `‖E g‖`, rather than infer estimator variance from per-step norm dispersion.

**Evidence:** [`DG-0005 analysis`](../studies/DG-0005/analysis.md), [`confirmation_result.json`](../studies/DG-0005/confirmation_result.json), [`noise_shape_result.json`](../studies/DG-0005/noise_shape_result.json), confirmation commit `8032a937`, post-hoc pre-registration commit `2ac7f3d`.

## 4. Key Scientific Findings

### Finding 1 — Raw pairwise transfer was mostly optimizer exposure, not task semantics

**Observation:** ER←KS and ER←SI appeared to improve by 34.11 and 27.94 percentage points under same-epoch pair-vs-single comparisons. Step-matched ER-only controls reduced the residuals to `+0.53pp` with a zero-spanning interval and `−5.89pp`, respectively.

**Finding:** Same-epoch transfer matrices are invalid semantic-transfer evidence when task-set changes alter optimizer updates and effective task minibatches.

**Evidence level:** **Strong diagnostic** (`DG-0001`, 10-fold LOSO plus exposure controls).

**Why it matters:** Apparent directed transfer can be created by training opportunity. Asymmetric mechanisms cannot be justified from the raw matrix.

### Finding 2 — Persistent gradient conflict is not the supported explanation

**Observation:** Across seeds `0–4`, no task pair met the persistent-conflict threshold; late mean cosines were approximately zero.

**Finding:** Under matched `smp` 25-layer training, the tasks approach gradient near-orthogonality rather than persistent opposition.

**Evidence level:** **Confirmed diagnostic** (`DG-0002`).

**Why it matters:** Conflict-only gradient surgery or a dynamic relation method has no measured conflict target here.

### Finding 3 — MTRL does not regulate the reproducible gradient-scale signal

**Observation:** Baseline ER-to-smallest norm ratios were `7.243 ± 0.272` middle and `8.962 ± 0.456` late; MTRL remained `7.129 ± 0.421` and `9.004 ± 1.059`, with paired intervals including zero.

**Finding:** Classical MTRL's learned Ω does not mitigate shared-gradient norm imbalance under the standard mixture.

**Evidence level:** **Confirmed diagnostic** (`DG-0002`, F9), later refined by F10.

**Why it matters:** Head-parameter covariance and optimization scale are not interchangeable quantities.

### Finding 4 — Gradient scale is conditioned by the training mixture

**Observation:** Increasing ER's share from about 47 to 435 examples per batch reduced the late max/min ratio from `8.962` to `2.352` in `5/5` matched seeds, with the change localized to ER.

**Finding:** The observed ER gradient-scale dominance is a property of batch composition, not a task-intrinsic property at this representation.

**Evidence level:** **Confirmed** for mixture dependence (`DG-0005`, F10).

**Why it matters:** Any claim about “task gradient scale” must state the sampling regime. F9 can no longer justify a mechanism using an intrinsic-ER-scale premise.

### Finding 5 — The residual late-phase mechanism is narrower than first assumed

**Observation:** Under `1/√n` scaling, estimator size can account for the middle drop but at most about 76% of the late log-drop. Per-step norm dispersion is unusable as an estimator-noise proxy (`CV ≈ 0.32` versus an isotropic ceiling of `≈0.00057`).

**Interpretation:** At least about 20% of the late drop requires a smaller mean gradient or steeper-than-`1/√n` noise growth, but the analysis does not identify which.

**Evidence level:** **Preliminary / post-hoc constraint**, pre-registered before computation; not a new confirmed finding.

**Why it matters:** A future diagnostic should measure the pool-mean gradient `‖E g‖` late in training, not repeat the composition sweep or infer variance from norm dispersion.

## 5. Hypotheses Rejected or Weakened

1. **Hypothesis:** the raw ER-directed gains demonstrate beneficial asymmetric semantic transfer.  
   **Evidence:** update-matched ER-only controls reproduced nearly all KS-associated gain and more than all SI-associated gain.  
   **Consequence:** `DG-0001` cannot motivate an asymmetric method; exposure-controlled transfer is required.

2. **Hypothesis:** persistent pairwise gradient conflict explains classical MTRL's null outcome.  
   **Evidence:** no pair met the pre-registered conflict threshold in any of five seeds; late gradients were near-orthogonal.  
   **Consequence:** do not prioritize conflict-only or dynamic-conflict mechanisms from this evidence.

3. **Hypothesis:** a published explicit Task Relation Learning method already provides a faithful direct fix for F9's gradient-scale symptom.  
   **Evidence:** eight primary sources failed at least one pre-registered gate; direct scale methods were optimization/loss weighting, while relation methods did not directly regulate the signal or required incompatible output structure.  
   **Consequence:** the F9-specific literature gate is closed and must not be repeated without new evidence or a scope change.

4. **Hypothesis:** ER is intrinsically a 7–9× larger-gradient task at this representation.  
   **Evidence:** changing only the training mixture removed late dominance in `5/5` matched seeds.  
   **Consequence:** the task-intrinsic scale justification for a relation/reliability mechanism is withdrawn.

5. **Weakened, not fully rejected:** classical MTRL fails specifically because useful relations are asymmetric.  
   **Evidence:** the measured asymmetry was exposure-confounded, and no beneficial controlled asymmetry survived.  
   **Consequence:** symmetry remains unresolved rather than established as the cause.

## 6. Current Understanding of MTRL

### Supported

- Classical MTRL has no meaningful demonstrated advantage over the matched wavCSE baseline under the completed multi-seed and ER LOSO protocols.
- Ω is conditioned by representation, seed/fold axis, and saturation state; it is not an intrinsic truth about KS/SI/ER.
- Ω magnitude saturates near `±1/3` under the main 25-layer `smp` condition, losing pair discrimination.
- Stable or large Ω entries do not imply useful transfer.
- Same-epoch pairwise transfer is confounded by optimizer exposure.
- Persistent pairwise conflict is absent under the matched 25-layer protocol.
- The large ER gradient norm is training-mixture dependent; MTRL does not regulate it.

### Weakened

- Beneficial task asymmetry as the demonstrated reason MTRL fails.
- Persistent gradient conflict as the main failure mechanism.
- An intrinsic ER scale/reliability property as the rationale for a new relation mechanism.
- The claim that a faithful published F9-specific relation method was ready to implement.
- Generic MTRL retuning: a stronger λ and longer training had already failed historically.

### Unresolved

- Whether Ω saturation causes the performance null or merely accompanies it.
- Whether the class-mean head summary destroys relation information.
- Whether relation-estimate confidence/noise changes under matched task composition.
- Whether useful controlled transfer is sparse, selective, or directional after full exposure matching.
- Whether the unexplained late ER norm component is a smaller mean gradient from faster convergence/overfitting or non-`1/√n` noise scaling.
- Whether a different published relation estimator or a clearly labelled project-original directed relation can beat both controls.

## 14. One-Paragraph Supervisor Summary

This week we moved from knowing that classical MTRL does not beat wavCSE to a much sharper explanation of what the existing diagnostics do and do not mean. `DG-0001` showed that the apparently large directed gains into emotion recognition were almost entirely caused by unequal optimizer exposure, so they cannot justify an asymmetric mechanism. `DG-0002` then confirmed across five seeds that ER has much larger shared-gradient norms under the standard training mixture and that MTRL neither mitigates this scale difference nor resolves any persistent pairwise conflict. The targeted literature gate found no faithful explicit relation method that directly addresses that scale symptom, and `DG-0005` subsequently showed why: increasing ER's per-batch representation reduced the late norm ratio from `8.96` to `2.35` in all five seeds, proving that the dominant scale signal is mixture-dependent rather than task-intrinsic. No architecture beat the matched wavCSE baseline, and no ER performance claim is made from the speaker-leaky split. By the reporting cutoff, the programme had therefore shifted—by explicit human decision—to a fixed benchmark of alternative relation-learning variants; the immediate work is to screen a verified p-MSSL estimator arm and a clearly labelled project-original directed-relation arm against both classical MTRL and wavCSE before any multi-seed or LOSO promotion.

## 15. Presentation-Ready Briefing

The following slide sequence is designed for a supervisor or research-group presentation. Each slide has one message, the evidence to display, and a concise speaking point.

### Slide 1 — Project and research goal

**Message:** We study whether learned task relations can improve joint speech learning across KS, SI, and ER using fixed WavLM features.

**Show:**

```text
Fixed WavLM embeddings
        ↓
shared downstream network
        ↓
KS (12) | SI (1251) | ER (4)
        ↓
learned task-relation mechanism
```

**Speaking point:** The representation is fixed so the independent variable is the relation mechanism. A successful method must beat matched wavCSE reproducibly without harming any task.

### Slide 2 — Why classical MTRL was not enough

**Message:** Classical MTRL is the formal starting point, but it has no reproducible performance advantage.

**Show:**

- 25-layer five-seed aggregate Δ: `−0.056pp` MTRL vs wavCSE;
- 16-layer five-seed aggregate Δ: `−0.010pp`;
- ER LOSO `opt` Δ: `−0.11pp`, 95% CI crossing zero;
- Ω often saturates near `±1/3`.

**Speaking point:** The problem was not “find a larger single-seed number”; it was to determine which MTRL assumption fails.

### Slide 3 — What was executed this week

**Message:** Four Studies completed by the cutoff, one began, and 38 accepted MLflow runs produced controlled diagnostic evidence.

**Show:**

|Study|Purpose|Scale|Decision|
|---|---|---:|---|
|DG-0001|Directed transfer + exposure controls|14 runs / 50 LOSO folds|Strong diagnostic; asymmetry weakened|
|DG-0002|Gradient compatibility|12 runs / 5 matched seeds|Confirmed scale; conflict rejected|
|LT-0001|F9-targeted literature gate|8 sources|Rejected|
|DG-0005|ER mixture intervention|12 runs / 5 matched seeds|Confirmed diagnostic|
|LT-0002|Variant source gate|Started 23:40 local|Active at cutoff|

**Speaking point:** The run count is not the contribution; the controlled elimination of plausible explanations is.

### Slide 4 — Analytics insight 1: apparent transfer was optimization exposure

**Message:** Raw transfer looked strongly directional, but the controlled residual did not.

**Show:**

|Direction|Raw ER gain|Exposure-matched residual|
|---|---:|---:|
|ER←KS|`+34.11pp`|`+0.53pp`, CI crosses zero|
|ER←SI|`+27.94pp`|`−5.89pp`, CI entirely negative|

**Speaking point:** The KS control explains about 98% of the raw gain; the SI control explains more than 100%, reversing the conclusion. Equal epochs were not equal optimization opportunity.

### Slide 5 — Analytics insight 2: conflict was not the mechanism

**Message:** The tasks are mostly near-orthogonal late in training, not persistently opposed.

**Show:**

- no pair met the conflict threshold in any of five seeds;
- late baseline mean cosines: KS↔SI `+0.002`, KS↔ER `+0.001`, SI↔ER `+0.022`;
- MTRL did not produce a resolved accuracy improvement.

**Speaking point:** This rules out persistent pairwise conflict as the supported explanation and removes the basis for conflict-only methods.

### Slide 6 — Analytics insight 3: gradient scale changed with the mixture

**Message:** ER's 7–9× norm dominance was real under the standard sampler, but it was not intrinsic to ER.

**Show:**

|Phase|Standard mixture|ER-weighted mixture|
|---|---:|---:|
|Middle ratio|`7.243`|`2.818`|
|Late ratio|`8.962`|`2.352`|
|ER examples / batch|`≈47`|`≈435`|
|Late threshold passed|0/5 below 3|5/5 below 3|

**Speaking point:** ER's late norm fell about 77%; KS and SI barely moved. Task-gradient scale must therefore be reported with sampling composition.

### Slide 7 — What the post-hoc analysis adds

**Message:** Sampling explains the middle-phase drop, but cannot explain all of the late-phase drop under the stated noise model.

**Show:**

- expected sampling ratio: about `3.0×`;
- observed late ER norm ratio: `3.99–4.69×`;
- estimator-size share bound: `0.758 [0.715, 0.801]`;
- at least about 20% of the late drop remains attributable to a smaller mean gradient or steeper noise scaling.

**Speaking point:** This is a bounded post-hoc insight, not a confirmed new finding. The next diagnostic measurement, if needed, is `‖E g‖` late in training.

### Slide 8 — What was ruled out

**Message:** Negative results narrowed the research space.

**Show:**

1. raw beneficial asymmetry from same-epoch transfer — rejected;
2. persistent gradient conflict — rejected;
3. task-intrinsic ER gradient dominance — rejected;
4. ready-made faithful F9-specific relation mechanism — rejected;
5. classical MTRL as a reproducible winner — already rejected by matched evaluation.

**Speaking point:** Each negative result removed a plausible but invalid motivation for the next architecture.

### Slide 9 — Current performance and evidence standard

**Message:** The matched wavCSE baseline remains champion; no new method result exists yet.

**Show:**

- no reproducible aggregate win;
- no authoritative ER win;
- single-seed results remain screening only;
- ER claims require 10-fold LOSO;
- every variant must beat both MTRL and wavCSE, with no task regression over `0.20pp`.

**Speaking point:** We are preserving claim quality rather than promoting a diagnostic intervention or leaky ER number as performance.

### Slide 10 — Direction and next experiment

**Message:** The project has moved to a controlled variant benchmark, not an open-ended architecture search.

**Show:**

```text
p-MSSL — published sparse precision estimator
        vs
classical MTRL + matched wavCSE

project-original directed relation
        vs
classical MTRL + matched wavCSE
```

**Speaking point:** p-MSSL is the clean published candidate identified post-cutoff. The directed arm is explicitly project-original under `DEC-0014`, not a claimed reproduction of AMTL, AutoTR, or GAMTL. Each arm screens once, then only promising candidates move to five seeds and ER LOSO.

### Recommended presentation headline

> **This week did not produce a new champion; it produced a defensible causal narrowing. Apparent directed transfer was an exposure artifact, persistent conflict was absent, and ER gradient dominance was shown to depend on the training mixture. That evidence now supports a tightly controlled benchmark of alternative relation estimators and directed relations.**
