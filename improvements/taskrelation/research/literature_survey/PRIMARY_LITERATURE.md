# Primary-literature ledger — task relations in wavCSE

Verification: 2026-09-29. `PRIMARY` means the linked *paper itself* was read (title, relevant equation and qualification checked), not merely its abstract, survey description or an existing repository card. `SURVEY` is a map, never a substitute for a primary source. Confidence is in the attribution, **not** confidence that the method improves wavCSE. Links here are source citations; repo evidence is indexed below. Where the literal primary object is replaced by a head summary, that is an **adaptation**, regardless of the existing in-category comparator doing the same.

| Claim / interpretation | Source, location and status | Confidence / wavCSE consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parameter-based MTL includes low-rank, task clustering, task-relation learning and decomposition as distinct categories; feature learning is separate. | [Zhang & Yang, *A Survey on Multi-Task Learning*, arXiv:1707.08114v3 (2021)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.08114), §2 opening, pp. 2–3; **SURVEY**. | High taxonomy confidence; scope boundaries bind. |
| Task-relation methods model covariance and similarity, not automatically observed transfer; survey explicitly describes sparse covariance and an asymmetric parameter-reconstruction relation. | Same survey, §2.4, Eqs. (20)–(25), pp. 8–9; **SURVEY**. | High as survey map; equations checked against primary below. Survey's §2.4 says `(I−A)(I−A)^T` is symmetric even when `A` is asymmetric. |
| Matrix-normal prior over aligned `W∈R^{d×m}`; trace-constrained PSD covariance `C`; `tr(W C^{-1} W^T)` and `C=√(W^TW)/tr√(W^TW)`. | [Zhang & Yeung, *A Convex Formulation for Learning Task Relationships in Multi-Task Learning*, UAI 2010](https://event.cwi.nl/uai2010/papers/UAI2010_0144.pdf), §2.1 Eq. (3),(7), §2.2 Eq. (13), pp. 2–4; **PRIMARY**. | High mathematical attribution. Convexity belongs to paper's shallow convex loss, **not** downstream deep neural net. It is the historical MTRL control and not a novel successor. |
| Their 'asymmetric MTL' means *a new target arriving after existing source tasks*, while the enlarged covariance remains symmetric. | Same UAI 2010 paper, §1 and §2.3, pp. 1, 4–5; **PRIMARY**. | High; never cite as a learned directed graph. |
| p-MSSL treats aligned feature-row task coefficients as approximately Gaussian, learns sparse **precision** `P=Σ^{-1}`; `P_ij=0` is *conditional* independence given other tasks; `tr(W P W^T)−d log|P|+λ₂‖P‖₁`. | [Gonçalves, Von Zuben & Banerjee, *Multi-task Sparse Structure Learning with Gaussian Copula Models*, JMLR 17(33), 2016](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume17/15-215/15-215.pdf), §3.1–3.3 Eqs. (3),(4b), pp. 6–8; **PRIMARY**. | High for model; at three tasks a precision zero can coexist with nonzero marginal covariance. Existing TR-0007 tests this family; no duplicate study. |
| Its **equivalent** graphical-lasso step is `λ₀ tr(SP)−log|P|+(λ₂/d)‖P‖₁`, `S=W^TW/d`; ADMM spectral/soft-threshold updates. Paper Eqs. (3),(8),(11) write element-wise norm/threshold and do not explicitly exempt the diagonal; repository DEC-0015 adopts off-diagonal-only graphical-lasso convention. Do **not** mix Eq. (8)'s normalized trace with Eq. (4b)'s unnormalized `d log|P|`. | Same JMLR paper, §3.3.1 Eq. (8)–(11), pp. 9–10, Algorithm 1 p. 8; **PRIMARY**. | High for equations, diagonal convention must be declared. λ₂ chosen on validation in paper; DEC-0016 fixes 0.01 for project screen, not a paper default. |
| r-MSSL learns conditional structure of **residual errors**, unlike p-MSSL; classification residuals are not Gaussian under its model. | Same JMLR paper, §3.7 Eq. (21)–(22), pp. 14–15; **PRIMARY**. | High; regression-only as published; disjoint multiclass KS/SID/ER need a project-original likelihood/residual construction. |
| SPATS puts an `ℓ₁` penalty on the **task covariance** `C`, not on precision; a zero `C_ij` removes a term in the paper's representer expansion under its own assumptions. | [Zhang & Yang, *Learning Sparse Task Relations in Multi-Task Learning*, AAAI 2017](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/10820/10679), §2–3 Eq. (3), Theorem 1 and Eqs. (4)–(5), pp. 2–3; **PRIMARY**. | High for shallow model. Here shared trunk still transfers across tasks even if a relation edge is zero; zero is not a global no-transfer intervention. Same mean-head adapter would be an adaptation. |
| SPATS reports weak results on a **four-task** sentiment dataset relative to dense methods and proposes insufficient transfer under sparse coupling as one explanation; not a proof that sparsity always hurts at three tasks. | Same AAAI 2017 paper, Experiments on Real-World Datasets, Table 2 and following discussion, pp. 5–6; **PRIMARY**. | High for reported outcome, low for projecting it onto KS/SID/ER. |
| AMTL learns nonnegative directed `B_st` reconstructing target `w_t` from source `w_s` and penalizes source outgoing-row mass by its **own** training loss; nonnegativity is task-*weight* sign, not a signed negative-transfer edge. | [Lee, Yang & Hwang, *Asymmetric Multi-task Learning Based on Task Relatedness and Loss*, ICML 2016](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/leeb16.pdf), §3 Eq. (1)–(3), Algorithm 1, pp. 3–4; **PRIMARY**. | High for model; published task vectors are equal-dimensional, positive relatedness assumed. Multiclass heads of 12/1251/4 cannot enter `W−WB` directly. |
| AMTL Theorem 1 has **two** alternatives: greater outgoing mass implies lower own loss **or** exchanging row masses worsens the reconstruction penalty. It does not establish an unconditional loss ordering or causal directed benefit. | Same ICML 2016 paper, §3 Theorem 1 pp. 3–4; **PRIMARY**. | High; loss imbalance itself differs with multiclass label entropy and ER per-batch composition. |
| AMTL's `‖W−WB‖²_F = tr[W(I−B)(I−B)^TW^T]` induces a symmetric effective quadratic precision, while `B` remains an asymmetric *reconstruction coefficient*. | Same ICML Eq. (1) p. 3; survey §2.4 Eq. (25) p. 9; **PRIMARY + SURVEY**. | High algebraic identity; a fitted directed coefficient does not alone demonstrate directed outcome transfer. |
| Higher-order relation uses `(W^TW)^t∼Wishart(d,C)` and `C=(W^TW)^t/d`; for `t=2`, `(G²)_ij=Σ_k G_ik G_kj` counts mediated similarity, not directional effect. It assumes full-rank Gram to use log determinant. | [Zhang & Yeung, *Learning High-Order Task Relationships in Multi-Task Learning*, IJCAI 2013](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/13/Papers/283.pdf), §2.1–2.4 Eqs. (2)–(3),(8)–(10), pp. 2–4; **PRIMARY**. | High; m=3 leaves only one distinct third task per pair; summary-rank collapse and a Gaussian regression likelihood make porting high risk. Not a new higher-order three-way *causal interaction*. |
| GO-MTL factors aligned task coefficients `W=LS` with sparse task-to-latent-basis columns; overlap of support induces sharing, not a learned task covariance or directed causal edge. `k<T`, so for T=3 only k=1 or 2. | [Kumar & Daumé III, *Learning Task Grouping and Overlap in Multi-task Learning*, ICML 2012](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.6417), §3 Eq. (1)–(3), pp. 2–4; **PRIMARY**. | High; valid alternative mechanism in the survey's clustering/low-rank category, **out of this branch**. |
| GAMTL estimates a separate nonnegative directed reconstruction `B^g` per externally meaningful covariate group and group-Lasso regularizes parameters; it is not a task cluster. | [de Oliveira, Gonçalves & Von Zuben, *Group LASSO with Asymmetric Structure Estimation for Multi-Task Learning*, IJCAI 2019](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/2019/0444.pdf), §3 Eq. (1)–(5), Algorithm 1, pp. 2–4; **PRIMARY**. | High for published group-conditioned direction; mean heads **and** an invented hidden-coordinate partition would be two adaptations, not a faithful wavCSE method. |
| Cross-stitch has a free 2×2 activation mixing matrix, potentially asymmetric, but shares **features** at identically-shaped network layers, not per-task parameter-covariance. | [Misra et al., *Cross-stitch Networks for Multi-task Learning*, CVPR 2016](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2016/papers/Misra_Cross-Stitch_Networks_for_CVPR_2016_paper.pdf), §3.3 Eq. (1)–(3); **PRIMARY**. | High; unequal logits and disjoint examples preclude literal head stitch; separate §2.1 feature-sharing family. |
| PCGrad projects a task gradient only when its dot product with another task gradient is negative; it learns no persistent parameter-relation matrix. | [Yu et al., *Gradient Surgery for Multi-Task Learning*, 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.06782), §2.2–2.3 Algorithm 1; **PRIMARY**. | High for object classification; DG-0002's absence of persistent negative cosines argues against conflict-only surgery here; not Task Relation Learning §2.4. |
